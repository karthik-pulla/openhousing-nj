Which New Jersey towns are actually affordable?

OpenHousing is a free, open tool that scores all 564 New Jersey municipalities on what it costs to own or rent a home, compared with what people in the region earn. Every number comes from a public source.

How it works

Public data in, two scores out

Every town gets a score from 0 to 100 for owning and another for renting. Higher means more affordable.

1

We gather public data

Every month from the U.S. Census Bureau, HUD, the NJ Department of Community Affairs and Freddie Mac, matched to towns by official ID.

2

We compare cost with income

The yearly cost of owning (mortgage plus property tax) or renting the typical home, as a share of the county's median household income.

3

Each town is ranked

Towns are ranked against each other, and each score says how reliable it is. Nothing is published unless every check passes.

Two markets, two scores. Owning and renting are almost separate markets, so they're scored separately and never combined. Zoning and the state's affordable-housing obligations are shown as context, but never change a score.
